# Computational Physics: Phase Ordering Kinetics

A study of phase transitions, phase separation, and domain growth in physical systems.

## Overview

This project studies the evolution of initially disordered systems toward ordered states following a rapid quench. It focuses on the formation and growth of domains, domain-wall dynamics, and the differences between conserved and non-conserved order parameters.

The theoretical framework is based on the study of phase-ordering kinetics and the mathematical models used to describe the evolution of phase-separating systems.

## Topics Covered

- Phase transitions and the ferromagnetic Ising model
- Phase separation in binary liquids
- Landau free energy and the order parameter
- Scaling hypothesis and characteristic domain length
- Domain walls, curvature, and surface tension
- Allen–Cahn equation for non-conserved dynamics
- Cahn–Hilliard equation for conserved dynamics
- Diffusion, chemical potential, and the Gibbs–Thomson boundary condition

## Mathematical Framework

The project discusses two important models of domain evolution:

**Non-conserved dynamics:** Described by the time-dependent Ginzburg–Landau framework and associated with curvature-driven domain-wall motion.

**Conserved dynamics:** Described by the Cahn–Hilliard equation, where domain growth occurs through diffusion of the conserved order parameter.
